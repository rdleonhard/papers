# Complex Life as Constructed Substrate

## A testable hypothesis of prokaryotic genome engineering and the Cambrian radiation

Oct 3, 2026 · @Rob

## Abstract

This paper states a hypothesis, assembles the mechanistic precedents for it, and specifies the measurements that would confirm or kill it. The hypothesis: prokaryotes evolved machinery that heritably modifies the germline genomes of simple multicellular hosts, biasing change toward more capable bodies, and did so by ordinary natural selection because a better host is a better vehicle. On this account complex life is not an independent achievement but a constructed substrate, built to concentrate nutrients, transport colonies, exclude rival colonies and resist predators. Its obligate dependence on its microbial passengers is then not incidental but definitional. The explanatory target is the Cambrian radiation, where body plans appear across a window of roughly 541 to 514 million years ago that the standard multi-causal account, shelf oxygenation plus new ecological interactions plus regulatory repatterning, treats as necessary but acknowledges as insufficient.

The critical distinction from existing symbiosis theory is that nothing is donated. The hypothesis proposes biased mutagenesis and recombination of host DNA, so the product is ordinary host sequence carrying no foreign signature. Horizontal-transfer evidence, favourable or unfavourable, therefore does not test it, and neither does the hologenome literature on microbially shifted phenotypes.

Every component the mechanism requires is documented somewhere in biology, which is unusual for a proposed hidden process. Bacteria regulate their own mutation rates under second-order selection. Diversity-generating retroelements perform targeted hypermutation confined to a single chosen locus. IS110 bridge recombinases join a programmable target to a programmable donor. Gene transfer agents are domesticated phage particles under host regulatory control, and tailocins are domesticated phage deployed as weapons against competitors, which supplies a plausible origin route from viral weapon to delivery vehicle to editor. *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* already performs heritable genome modification on a multicellular eukaryote by targeting reproductive tissue. Bacteria already induce eukaryotic morphogenesis: a single *Algoriphagus* sulfonolipid triggers multicellular development in the closest living relatives of animals at femtomolar concentration. And the deepest version of the claim is established rather than speculative, because the eukaryotic cell is itself a prokaryotic construction whose energy budget made large genomes affordable.

What is missing is one number. The hypothesis predicts that the rate, spectrum or genomic distribution of de novo host germline mutations depends on microbial presence. No study comparing germline mutation rate and spectrum between axenic and colonised animals could be located. That measurement is cheap, and two further tests, mining vertically transmitted symbiont genomes for editing cassettes and comparing rates of adaptive evolution across clades with and without obligate endosymbionts, can be run on data that already exists. The paper closes with the obstacles the hypothesis must clear, chief among them that animals already run a domesticated transposase to generate adaptive diversity and confine it strictly to the soma.

## 1. The hypothesis

Complex multicellular life is a vehicle built by prokaryotes, by selection, for prokaryotic ends. The hypothesis has six claims, stated so that each can be attacked separately.

1. **Baseline.** Prokaryotic life is metabolically self-sufficient and, if life arises at all, is the ordinary and probably common state of it. Prokaryotes require no host. Complex life requires them.
2. **Competition.** Prokaryotic lineages compete for resources and space, and selection favours any heritable trait that improves resource capture or removes rivals. This is uncontroversial and is the engine the rest of the hypothesis runs on.
3. **The vehicle.** Among such traits is the construction of a multicellular eukaryotic body. A body concentrates nutrients, carries colonies into new territory, excludes competing colonies, and defends against organisms that would consume them. A motile, muscled, sensing animal is a better vehicle than a sessile mat.
4. **The mechanism.** Construction proceeds by heritable modification of host germline DNA: biased mutagenesis, recombination and rearrangement rather than gene donation. The bias is positional rather than semantic. The colony does not know what change would help; it concentrates change where change has historically paid, in the way that a diversity-generating retroelement concentrates mutation in a tail-fibre gene and nowhere else. The product is ordinary host sequence.
5. **The editor evolved.** This capability is not a side effect. It is an adaptation acquired by ordinary Darwinian means, because colonies that built better vehicles propagated more than colonies that did not.
6. **Obligate dependence.** It follows that complex life cannot persist in robust form without its colonies. Dependence is not an accident of long cohabitation. It is the designed condition of a substrate.

**What the hypothesis is not.** It is not a claim about intention, foresight or design. Claim 4 explicitly denies foresight: a positional bias requires no knowledge of the future, only a record of where variation has paid in the past. It is not the hologenome concept, which concerns host and microbiome as a unit of selection and the microbial shifting of host phenotypes, both of which leave host DNA untouched. It is not horizontal gene transfer, and this matters more than any other distinction in the paper. If the mechanism is mutagenic rather than donative, there is no foreign sequence to find, no donor phylogeny to reconstruct and no junction to sequence. Evidence about transferred genes, whether the transfers look functional or dead, is simply the wrong instrument.

**What it competes with.** The standard account of rapid adaptation holds that variation is generated without regard to fitness and that speed comes from the reservoir of standing variation plus developmental architecture that converts small genetic changes into large coherent phenotypic ones. The hypothesis does not deny that selection does the sorting. It contests where the raw material comes from, and specifically whether its production is passive.

## 2. What needs explaining: the Cambrian radiation

The explanatory target is the appearance of animal body plans in the Early Cambrian, concentrated around 530 to 520 million years ago within a window of roughly 541 to 514. The current synthesis is multi-causal: oxygenation of shallow shelves, the establishment of new ecological interactions including predation, and repatterning of gene regulatory networks ([Erwin & Valentine 2013](https://blog.nhbs.com/?p=7360)). The honest statement of its own limit is that the core developmental toolkit, including the Hox complex, was in place well before the radiation, so the existence of those networks is necessary but not sufficient. Something changed about how fast viable new configurations could be found.

The hypothesis offers a candidate for the sufficient cause, and locates it in the party that had the longer track record. By the Ediacaran, prokaryotes had been evolving for roughly three billion years, through a number of generations and a volume of genetic experimentation that no animal lineage has ever approached. They already possessed, and still possess, the full enzymatic repertoire for cutting, joining, reverse-transcribing and inserting DNA. Animals are latecomers using borrowed machinery. If anything in the biosphere had the capability and the time to evolve a genome-editing apparatus, it was the prokaryotes.

One version of this claim is not speculative but established, and it is the strongest single argument the hypothesis has. The eukaryotic cell is a prokaryotic construction. Mitochondria are domesticated bacteria, and the energetic consequence was decisive: by partitioning the DNA that must sit beside the respiratory machinery from the DNA that need not, eukaryotes achieved an energy budget per gene that lets an average eukaryotic cell support on the order of a hundred thousand times more genes than a bacterium can afford ([Lane & Martin 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09486)). Large genomes, large gene families and elaborate regulatory systems became purchasable only after that event. Complex life exists downstream of a prokaryotic acquisition.

So the question the hypothesis asks is not whether bacteria built complex life. They did, once, at the level of the cell. The question is whether the same thing happened again at the level of the body.

## 3. Bacteria already write DNA

The hypothesis asks for an evolved prokaryotic apparatus that modifies a genome in a targeted, heritable way. Every element of that description is documented, and in most cases has been for years. The inventory below is the hypothesis's main asset: it is not asking for a new kind of biochemistry, only for a known kind pointed at a new target.

| System | What it does | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stress-induced mutagenesis | Raises mutation rate under specific stresses, lowers it otherwise. Maintained by second-order selection, where the mutation-raising allele hitchhikes with the benefits it generates | Mutation production is a trait under selection, not a fixed background process ([Ram & Hadany 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5717628/)) |
| Diversity-generating retroelements | A reverse transcriptase and accessory protein make an error-prone cDNA from a template repeat and install it by mutagenic retrohoming, mutating adenines only, and only inside the host-recognition region of the phage tail fibre | Targeted hypermutation confined to one chosen locus is evolvable and exists. This is the closest analogue to the proposed mechanism ([Doulatov et al. 2004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3570691)) |
| Bridge recombinases (IS110) | A structured non-coding RNA with two independently programmable internal loops, one specifying target and one specifying donor, directs insertion, excision or inversion of sequence up to several kilobases | Programmable joining of chosen DNA pieces is native bacterial biochemistry ([Durrant, Perry et al. 2024](https://arcinstitute.org/news/bridge)) |
| Retrons | Reverse transcriptase plus non-coding RNA producing single-stranded DNA in vivo; engineered as donor templates for editing in eukaryotic cells | Bacteria manufacture DNA templates internally ([retron census 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10849725/)) |
| *Agrobacterium* T-DNA transfer | VirD2 relaxase nicks the T-DNA, VirE2 coats it, a type IV secretion system delivers it to the plant nucleus, and it integrates into the chromosome. In floral dip the target is the ovule and the change reaches the seed | A bacterium with a dedicated, selected apparatus performs heritable genome modification on a multicellular eukaryote by targeting reproductive tissue ([Clough & Bent 1998](https://home.csulb.edu/~bruss/documents/floraldip.pdf); [Desfeux et al. 2000](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/female-reproductive-tissues-are-the-primary-target-of-agrobacteri/)) |
| RAG1/RAG2 recombinase | Cuts and rejoins antibody and T-cell receptor gene segments to generate adaptive diversity. Derived from a Transib-superfamily transposase, with recognition sequences from that element's terminal repeats | Mobile-element machinery can be installed in an animal and run as a directed diversity generator. The device works in animal cells ([Transib co-evolution review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8355894/)) |

The last row is double-edged and the paper returns to it in section 10. Animals do run a domesticated transposase whose function is deliberate DNA cutting and rejoining to produce useful variation. Selection built it, and confined it to somatic cells at a handful of loci under heavy regulation, because off-target activity produces lymphoma. The capability is demonstrably compatible with animal biology. Its confinement is the central fact a proponent has to explain.

## 4. The viral route: how such a capability could originate and scale

A genome editor aimed at a host is a sophisticated end product, and a hypothesis is stronger if it can show a gradual path to it rather than demanding the finished article. The proposed path begins with something far smaller than a eukaryotic genome: a virus used as a weapon.

The first two steps of that path are established.

**Bacteria build phage-derived weapons.** Tailocins, also called phage tail-like bacteriocins, are contractile or flexible phage tail structures encoded in bacterial genomes and derived from prophages. Most *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* strains produce them as R-type and F-type pyocins, which puncture the membranes of competing strains. They are assembled at the cell centre, moved to the poles, and released by explosive self-lysis that disperses them tens of micrometres to reach rivals ([tailocin review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7815802); [deployment mechanism](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10294684)). This is a bacterium spinning up a virus-derived device to kill its competitors, which is precisely the first step the hypothesis posits, and it required no new speculation to find.

**Bacteria build phage-derived DNA delivery machines.** Gene transfer agents are phage-like particles encoded in bacterial genomes, best characterised in *Rhodobacter capsulatus*. The structural genes are phage-derived, but the phage genes for replication and expression control are gone, and the particle is produced under the bacterium's own regulatory circuits. They are described in the literature as domesticated, selfless viruses co-opted to serve their hosts. The particle resembles a tailed phage with a shortened head that limits packaging to under about 4,500 base pairs, and it ejects DNA through an opening constriction in the baseplate ([Bárdy et al. 2020, Nature Communications](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7296036/)).

So bacteria already domesticate phage for two purposes, killing and DNA delivery, and they regulate both. The hypothesis requires a third application: a delivery machine aimed at a eukaryotic germline. No phage-derived example of that is known. A non-phage example is, and it is *Agrobacterium*, which shows that the target is reachable and that reproductive tissue is a viable aim point.

The escalation the hypothesis proposes therefore runs: weapon against competitors, then delivery vehicle for the colony's own DNA, then editor of a host's DNA. Each of the first two steps is documented in extant bacteria. The composite is not. That is an honest statement of where the hypothesis sits: assembled from real parts, with the final assembly unobserved.

The practical consequence is that this section converts into the cheapest test in the paper. If the mechanism exists, the machinery should be present in the genomes of vertically transmitted symbionts, should show target specificity matching host sequence motifs rather than phage or plasmid motifs, and should be under purifying selection. Those genomes are already sequenced.

## 5. Bacteria already build eukaryotic bodies

The hypothesis needs bacteria to have shaped animal form. A weaker version of that is already established: bacteria routinely hold the switches that determine whether and how a eukaryotic body develops. Four cases carry the point.

**Multicellularity in the closest living relatives of animals is bacterially induced.** The choanoflagellate *Salpingoeca rosetta* develops into multicellular rosette colonies in response to a sulfonolipid, rosette-inducing factor 1, produced by its prey bacterium *Algoriphagus machipongonensis*. The factor is active at femtomolar concentration and the response spans nine orders of magnitude of dose. The authors state plainly that this suggests molecular mechanisms by which bacteria may have contributed to the evolution of animals ([Alegado et al. 2012, eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/00013)). Choanoflagellates are the sister group to animals, and the transition to multicellularity is the precondition for every body plan that followed. A bacterium controls it.

**A bacterium builds an organ.** *Vibrio fischeri* induces the morphogenesis of the bobtail squid light organ. Without colonisation the organ does not develop normally. The host supplies the genes; the bacterium supplies the instruction to use them.

**A basal animal stops reproducing without its microbiota.** Germ-free *Hydra* polyps show budding inhibition in 89 % of animals within four weeks, which is reversed by restoring native bacteria, and the microbiota influences stem cell decisions ([microbiota and *Hydra* stem cells](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.08.20.608462.full.pdf)). Germ-free polyps are also prone to fungal infection that resident bacteria suppress.

**The eukaryotic cell is a prokaryotic product.** This is the case discussed in section 2 and it remains the strongest. Mitochondrial endosymbiosis is what made large genomes affordable ([Lane & Martin 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09486)).

The limit of this evidence must be stated as plainly as the evidence itself. All four cases are induction, nutrition or energetics. A bacterium flipping a developmental switch is not a bacterium that wrote the switch. The hypothesis requires authorship, and these cases demonstrate only operation. They establish that bacteria are deeply embedded in the control of animal development, which makes authorship conceivable and makes the proposed relationship familiar rather than exotic. They do not demonstrate it.

## 6. The dependence prediction: complex life as obligate substrate

If complex life is a constructed vehicle, it should not be robustly viable without its crew. This is the hypothesis's most distinctive prediction and the easiest to assess against existing data, because the relevant observations have been accumulating for eighty years.

**The strongest cases are extreme.** *Riftia pachyptila*, the giant hydrothermal vent tubeworm, has no mouth, no gut and no digestive tract at any adult stage. It houses a single phylotype of sulphur-oxidising bacteria in specialised bacteriocytes of an internal organ, the trophosome, and depends on that symbiont completely for nutrition while supplying it with the inorganic substrates for chemosynthesis ([Riftia genome study](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.09.04.458960.full.pdf)). The animal is, functionally, a bacterial culture vessel with muscles and a circulatory system. Aphids deprived of *Buchnera* grow slowly and are reproductively sterile. Termites and ruminants cannot process their diets alone.

**The general case is impairment rather than death.** Germ-free mammals are viable but systematically degraded: immature lymphoid tissue, deficient mature lymphocytes, grossly enlarged caecum, altered brain volumes and microglial populations, altered myelination, reduced fecundity, slower development. Read through the hypothesis, this list is what a vehicle looks like with the crew removed. It still rolls; nothing works properly.

**The honest bound.** Three observations limit how strong the dependence claim can be. Germ-free rat and mouse colonies have been bred continuously since 1946 and 1959 respectively, so sterility is survivable across generations. Leaf-feeding caterpillars, sampled across 121 species in 15 families, have no resident gut microbiome, bacterial densities orders of magnitude below other insects, and show no measurable cost when their transient bacteria are suppressed with antibiotics ([Hammer et al. 2017, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/5594680)). Axenic *Drosophila* lines maintained for 54 to 57 generations with sterility verified each generation did not collapse and continued to respond to selection ([Malwade et al. 2024](https://biorxiv.org/cgi/content/short/2024.10.25.620247v1)). Not all animals need a microbiome, and the literature now frames reliance as a continuum rather than a universal ([Hammer, Sanders & Fierer 2019](https://www.fiererlab.org/s/Hammer_etal_2019_FEMSMicro.pdf)).

The hypothesis should therefore predict *which* lineages depend and which do not, rather than asserting universal dependence. A defensible version says: dependence should be deepest where symbionts are vertically transmitted and ancient, and shallowest where hosts evolved host-encoded substitutes for symbiont functions. Caterpillars fit that second category, since their alkaline, fast-transit gut and host-encoded detoxification enzymes are exactly what a lineage that dispensed with symbionts would need.

**The weapon exception, and a warning about it.** The proposal allows that some complex life might be maintained microbe-poor deliberately, as a weapon against a colony's competitors or predators. This is coherent, but it is also the kind of auxiliary clause that can absorb any disconfirming case. If a microbe-free animal counts as a colony's weapon, no microbe-free animal can ever count against the hypothesis. To keep the claim falsifiable, the exception needs independent criteria stated in advance: which colony, which competitor, and what observable the weapon relationship predicts beyond the absence it was invoked to explain.

## 7. The germline problem, egg proximity, and where to look

The hypothesis's hardest physical obstacle is access. In bilaterian animals the germline is set aside early in development and defended immunologically, so that somatic events do not reach the heritable genome. Weismann's separation of germline from soma is not a wall, and transgenerational epigenetic effects have eroded the strict version of it, but it remains the reason that a gut bacterium has no route to an animal's gametes.

Three considerations turn this obstacle into a set of directions for where to search.

**Lineages without the barrier.** Plants have no sequestered germline, which is exactly why *Agrobacterium* succeeds on them and why floral dip produces transgenic seed. Among animals, cnidarians, sponges and placozoans segregate germline late or draw gametes from somatic stem cell populations. This is a significant point in the hypothesis's favour, because the ancestral host the proposal nominates, a jellyfish-grade organism, belongs to the group with the fewest barriers. If a prokaryotic editor ever operated, the most permissive substrates available to it were precisely the simple diploblasts that preceded the bilaterian radiation.

**Symbionts are routinely present at eggs.** The proposal's timing, that editing happens when the host is dying with eggs still within reach of the colony, has real analogues. Vertical transmission through the oocyte is the normal route for *Wolbachia*, which is estimated to infect 52 % of terrestrial arthropod species, alongside *Rickettsia* at 24 % and *Cardinium* at 13 % ([Weinert et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4424649)). Stinkbugs smear symbiont-laden secretions onto egg surfaces; other insects package symbionts in capsules deposited with the clutch. Whatever else is true, symbionts are abundantly, reliably present at the moment and place where the next generation's genome is packaged. The window the mechanism requires is not hypothetical.

**The transmission requirement is satisfiable.** An editing lineage must capture the benefit of its own work, which means it must be transmitted with the host's offspring rather than acquired from the environment. Environmentally acquired gut flora cannot sustain the trait, because the editing colony would pay while unrelated bacteria in the offspring collected the return. Vertically transmitted endosymbionts escape this, and they are not rare. Roughly half of arthropod species carry one.

The search order that follows is specific. Look first in plants and their endophytes, then in cnidarians, placozoans and sponges, then in arthropods with ancient vertically transmitted symbionts, and last in vertebrates. If a microbially dependent mutational signature is found in vertebrates but not in plants, the hypothesis is in difficulty on its own reasoning, because vertebrates present the harder barrier.

## 8. Dormancy and the oxygen trigger

The hypothesis need not claim that the machinery arose at the Cambrian boundary. It can claim that the machinery arose earlier and could not be used until conditions permitted, which is a weaker and better-supported position.

The independent energetics argument supplies the reason. Before mitochondrial endosymbiosis and before atmospheric and shelf oxygenation, large genomes and large active bodies were simply unaffordable. The energy available per gene in a prokaryote does not support the regulatory apparatus that a body plan requires, and the resolution of that constraint was the acquisition of mitochondria ([Lane & Martin 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature09486)). Oxygenation of shallow shelves across the Ediacaran and Cambrian transition is part of the standard explanation of the radiation's timing and of its episodic pattern.

So a dormant capability is not special pleading in this case. It coincides with a documented constraint that lifted at the right moment, and the pattern it predicts, a long period of capability without expression followed by rapid expression once the energy budget allowed, is the same pattern that the oxygenation account already describes.

This yields a dating prediction that is testable with existing methods. If the machinery predates the radiation, phylogenetic reconstruction of candidate cassettes across symbiont lineages should place their origin in the Proterozoic, well before 541 million years ago, and should show them present in symbiont lineages associated with basal animal groups. If every candidate cassette turns out to be a recent acquisition confined to derived insect symbionts, the Cambrian claim fails while the general mechanism claim could still stand.

## 9. Predictions, tests, and the measurement that has never been made

The hypothesis's central empirical consequence is narrow enough to decide. If prokaryotes mutagenise host germlines, then the rate, the spectrum or the genomic distribution of de novo mutations in a host germline must depend on microbial presence. If all three are invariant, the mechanism does not operate in that host and the hypothesis is reduced to a claim about lineages not yet examined.

That measurement does not exist. Searches of the literature return germline mutation rates across many vertebrate species, microbiome effects on somatic and intestinal mutation accumulation, and extensive work on the mutation rates of gut bacteria themselves, but no comparison of host germline mutation rate or spectrum between axenic and colonised animals. The single most diagnostic quantity for this hypothesis has not been measured, in either direction, which means the hypothesis is currently open rather than either supported or refuted. Establishing that gap is this paper's principal contribution.

| Test | Design | Hypothesis predicts | Null predicts | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Symbiont genome mining | Search *Wolbachia*, *Buchnera*, *Rickettsia*, *Cardinium* and plant endophyte genomes for diversity-generating retroelement cassettes, bridge recombinases, retrons and nucleases whose target specificity matches host motifs | Cassettes present, under purifying selection, with host-matched rather than phage-matched specificity | Absent, or present only as phage defence systems with phage-matched specificity | Analysis only; genomes already public |
| Comparative phylogenetics | Rates of adaptive molecular evolution and lineage diversification in arthropod clades with versus without ancient vertically transmitted endosymbionts, controlling for body size, generation time and effective population size | Elevated rates in symbiont-bearing clades | No association once confounders are controlled | Analysis only; genomes already public |
| Mutation-accumulation lines | 50 single-pair lines per arm, arms being axenic, gnotobiotic with a defined community, and conventional; 30 generations; whole-genome sequencing of founders and endpoints | Rate differs between arms, or spectrum shifts toward indels and structural variants, or mutations cluster non-randomly at regulatory and developmental loci in colonised arms | Identical rate, identical spectrum, uniform distribution | Moderate; *Drosophila* or *C. elegans* |
| Trio sequencing | Parent-offspring trios, axenic versus conventional, deep long-read coverage of DNA from dissected gonads or surface-sterilised embryos only | Excess of de novo events in colonised trios, concentrated in functional regions | No difference | Moderate |
| Plant versus animal asymmetry | The same mutation-accumulation design in a plant with and without its endophyte community | Effect larger in the plant, where germline access is unobstructed | No effect in either | Moderate |
| Dating | Phylogenetic reconstruction of any candidate cassette found in test 1 | Origin in the Proterozoic, before 541 Ma, present in symbionts of basal animal lineages | Recent origin confined to derived insect symbionts | Analysis only |

**Sampling discipline.** All host DNA must come from dissected gonads or surface-sterilised embryos, never whole animals, so that any bacterial read is either germline-integrated or contamination. The axenic arm sets the contamination floor. This precaution is not optional: every large claim of horizontal transfer into an animal genome that was later withdrawn, including the reported 17 % in a tardigrade, failed because non-axenic cultures put bacterial reads into the assembly.

**Decision rule.** Tests 1 and 2 are cheap and should run first, because a negative result in both would make the expensive work hard to justify, while a positive result in either would make it urgent. Tests 3 and 4 are decisive for the mechanism. A single replicated finding that germline mutation spectrum depends on microbial status, with the effect concentrated at functional loci, would establish the hypothesis's core claim and would be a major result independent of the Cambrian framing.

## 10. What the hypothesis must overcome

Six objections stand, and a proponent should engage them rather than route around them.

**Evolution built this device and kept it out of the germline.** The RAG recombinase is a domesticated transposase that cuts and rejoins DNA to generate adaptive diversity in animal cells, so the capability is compatible with animal biology. It is confined to somatic cells, to a handful of loci, under regulation whose failure causes lymphoma. Selection had the parts, had the opportunity, and did not point them at the heritable genome. The most natural reading is that germline mutagenesis is strongly deleterious on average, which is also what the mutation-load literature predicts.

**The one bacterium with reliable germline access does not edit.** *Wolbachia* occupies the oocyte cytoplasm in roughly half of arthropod species, which makes it the best-placed candidate editor in nature. Its documented effects on hosts are reproductive manipulation: cytoplasmic incompatibility, feminisation, parthenogenesis induction. Its nuclear insertions, where they occur, are pseudogenised and transcriptionally silent. It manipulates without writing.

**Microbe-free lineages are not evolutionarily impaired.** Caterpillars have no resident gut microbiome and Lepidoptera is nonetheless the second most diverse insect order. Axenic flies continue responding to selection across 57 generations. A field population of *Drosophila* evolved insecticide resistance in three generations, and when the evolved populations were reared axenically and had their microbiomes swapped, resistance tracked the host genotype rather than the microbiome.

**The information problem is only half-solved.** Positional bias escapes the need for foresight, but it also weakens the claim. A mechanism that concentrates mutation where mutation has historically paid produces faster search, not favourable mutation. That is a real and interesting claim, and it is a smaller one than the hypothesis began with.

**The lag problem is unmodelled.** The benefit of an edit reaches the editing lineage only after host generations have elapsed, which dilutes the selection coefficient on the editing trait by a factor that depends on host generation time and transmission fidelity. No model of this exists. Until one does, claim 5 is an assertion that the trait is evolvable rather than a demonstration.

**Parsimony.** Standing variation, recombination and developmental architecture already account for the observed cases of rapid complex adaptation that have been traced to their genetic cause. The hypothesis is not needed to explain them. It would be needed only if the mutational input itself proved to be microbially modulated, which returns to the measurement in section 9.

**Falsifiability discipline.** Two moves available to a proponent would make the hypothesis untestable, and both should be refused in advance. The first is counting transient, food-borne bacteria as editors, which makes every animal colonised and removes absence as an observation. The second is the weapon exception of section 6, which can absorb any microbe-free counterexample. A hypothesis that cannot be embarrassed by data is not in competition with the standard account; it is a different kind of statement.

## 11. Side note: the self-replicating constructor corollary

This section is a corollary, not evidence, and it is kept separate so that it places no weight on the empirical claims above.

If claims 1 through 5 hold, prokaryotes function as self-replicating constructors in the sense von Neumann described: entities that reproduce themselves and also build apparatus to act on their environment. Under the hypothesis the apparatus they build includes bodies, and the bodies become progressively more capable because more capable vehicles propagate colonies further. On arrival at a habitable world, such a lineage would reproduce, modify the chemistry it finds, and then construct increasingly elaborate hosts, with intelligence appearing late as one more vehicle refinement. Complex life would be an expected output of prokaryotic colonisation rather than a rare accident, and the apparent difficulty of the eukaryotic transition would be a constraint on timing rather than on probability.

Three cautions apply. First, this adds nothing testable: it makes the same predictions as the hypothesis proper, because every observation available to us comes from one planet. Second, it is not required by the hypothesis, which could be true on Earth as a local contingency. Third, framing of this kind attracts the objection that the argument is teleological. It is not, provided claim 4 holds: a positional mutational bias selected because it historically paid requires no goal, and a constructor that builds better vehicles over time is doing what any ratchet of variation and selection does. The corollary should be regarded as a consequence worth noticing, not a reason to believe the hypothesis.

## 12. Conclusion

The hypothesis is that prokaryotes evolved machinery to modify the germline genomes of simple hosts, that they did so because a more capable host is a more effective vehicle for a colony, and that this is why animal body plans appear as abruptly as they do in the Cambrian record. Its mechanism is biased mutagenesis rather than gene donation, so the product is ordinary host sequence and the horizontal-transfer literature does not bear on it.

The case for taking it seriously is that its components are not hypothetical. Bacteria regulate their own mutation rates under selection. They run targeted hypermutation machines confined to single loci. They encode programmable recombinases that join a chosen donor to a chosen target. They domesticate phage into weapons and into DNA delivery particles under their own regulatory control. One bacterium already performs heritable genome modification on a multicellular eukaryote by aiming at reproductive tissue. Bacteria induce multicellular development in the closest living relatives of animals, govern organ morphogenesis in animals, and built the eukaryotic cell outright. Vertically transmitted symbionts, which are the only candidates that could capture the benefit of editing, are present in roughly half of arthropod species and are reliably deposited at eggs. The lineages with the weakest germline barriers are the simple diploblasts that preceded the bilaterian radiation, which is where the hypothesis says the work was done.

The case against it is that animals already run a domesticated DNA-cutting recombinase and selection confined it to the soma, that the one bacterium with reliable germline access manipulates reproduction without editing genomes, that microbe-free lineages are evolutionarily successful, and that standing variation and developmental architecture already explain the rapid adaptations that have been traced to cause.

Between those two lists sits a single unmeasured quantity. Nobody has compared the germline mutation rate and spectrum of an axenic animal with a colonised one. Two further tests, mining symbiont genomes for editing cassettes and comparing rates of adaptive evolution across clades with and without ancient endosymbionts, require no new animals and no new sequencing. The hypothesis deserves those three measurements. If all three come back null, it should be abandoned for the lineages tested. If any one of them comes back positive, the question of where animal variation comes from is open in a way it has not been for a century.
